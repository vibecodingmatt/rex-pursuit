# Rex: Pursuit handoff

## Safari ground-animal motion (2026-09-26, local)

The user asked for less rigid running bodies and hanging arms, and for groups to stop stepping in sync, excluding compies. `safari-motion.js` now supplies size-appropriate heave, pitch and lateral weight transfer to the seven baked ground species (including the ghost raptor's shared rig). Lizards get a small height-weighted torso pulse as well as their existing spine bend. Compy and golden-compy gait settings are unchanged.

- **Suspension and feet:** the live instance pivots around the body centre. A separate `aBody` attribute lets the shader undo that transform at the toes, fading the correction up each leg. The planted half of the stride stays close to the road instead of inheriting the hip swing's upward arc. The heavy quadrupeds have smaller movement. Body motion must stay outside the old limb-weight blend: torso vertices have weight zero, which previously erased their bob.
- **Arms:** the existing sculpt generator now records part 4 with a shoulder pivot and smooth attachment weights, including fingers/nails. Both mesh tiers were rebuilt. The gait adds a delayed shoulder swing and a small outward movement; normals and depth shadows follow the pose. Shape, colour and topology remain the accepted sculpts.
- **Individuals:** each non-compy gets a persistent cadence multiplier (0.89–1.11); the baked animals also vary motion strength (0.9–1.1), and size adjusts cadence. Safari running speeds vary by 5%. Random starting phases remain. These variations are sampled on pool reuse, not every frame.
- **Hits and falls:** `livePose()` is shared by rendering, target positions and hit spheres. A kill carries the current body position/orientation into the fall, retaining the foot compensation as the death curl blends in, so a shot does not reset the runner's pose. The chase's shared Gallimimus herd gets the same improvements.
- **Checks:** `test:safari-motion` uses GPU transform feedback on the actual gait shader for all seven sculpts and both tiers: torso/arm movement, foot clearance, body targets, hits, continuous kill transforms, settled falls, herd cadence and compy exclusions. `test:safari`, `test:wildlife`, `test:smoke` and `build` pass. The before/after binary comparison confirms identical positions, normals, colours and triangle indices on both tiers; only rig data and its pivot tables changed. Before/after phase sheets, phone captures and the frame-cost comparison are in ignored `art/review/safari-motion/`.

## Spawns, roosts and snowy leaves (2026-09-26)

The user reported three bugs after the sculpt polish: Safari animals appearing and then running as if teleported, instead of coming out of the brush; flying reptiles clinging to nothing where no tree was; and the "snowy/white" leaves back by day and by night (their screenshot: verge leaves lit a flat pale blue at night).

- **Snowy leaves: the rim light.** The rim (`chase.js`) is a cool character light that outlines the animals, and it has no shadow map. It lit every leaf in the forest alike, deep under the canopy included: grey-white by day and in the storm, a flat blue frost by night. The 2026-09-24 fix left leaves a quarter of it at night and all of it by day. A/B against `65d3e55` showed identical frames, so this was not a regression, just not fully fixed. Turning each light's intensity to zero in a frozen view showed the rim alone makes the frost. Toggling visibility instead renumbers the light slots and misleads. Leaf materials (`plant()` with translucency) now leave directional light 1 (the rim) out of three's light loop entirely. `chase.js` notes that the rim must stay that slot, since the sun sorts first as the only shadow caster, and `foliage.js` throws if three's light chunk text changes. Moonlight reaches leaves through the moon's shadowed key light, so night foliage is dark masses under a moonlit sky; the Rex, road and trunks keep the rim.
- **Clinging to nothing.** Safari's Dimorphodon spawner perched it at x ±4.8, the road edge, where there is no tree (30% stayed there; 70% launched from mid-air). `flyers.huntSpawn` now takes a free roost on a visible edge tree 14–40 m back (either side). Most take off from it after a moment; with no roost in reach, one flies in across the road from behind the understory. Every perched Dimorphodon is now tied to its chunk and roost set (`c.home`) and is removed if the chunk wraps round, resets or gives its slot to the ford, so no swap can strand one. `art/review/bugs3/roosts.cjs` measures each perched one's distance to a real roost: a stray 10.8 m off before, none after (one sample at 139 m in opaque fog).
- **Pop-ins.** `critters.huntSpawn` started Safari crossers on the open verge (|x| 7.5, or 9 for the quadrupeds) at full speed. It now steps the start outward until the camera cannot see the animal (a frustum test with the hull radius) or it stands behind the concealing understory belt (16.5 for bipeds, 19 for quadrupeds, whose frills and plates overtop the belt). The start along the road allows for the extra run-in, signed so that on the slow title road animals no longer come closer than before. The crossing time scales to the distance. A crosser past its far verge keeps running until it leaves the view instead of shrinking in plain sight. Safari Pteranodon and Quetzalcoatlus now arrive out of the haze down the corridor (72–82 m and 92 m, as in the chase) instead of materialising at 42–58 m.
- **Checks:** `test:safari` (all kills, including the Quetzalcoatlus from farther out), `test:wildlife` (Dimorphodon roost-mate flush) and `test:smoke` pass. Captures and probes are in ignored `art/review/bugs3/`: `frost.cjs` (per-light removal), `snow-ab2.cjs` (frozen verge views on two builds; the old build is served from a worktree on 5191), `entry.cjs` (frames from the moment of a spawn), `spawns.cjs`, `roosts.cjs` and `pale.cjs` (pale-pixel hunt with raycast naming).

## Safari sculpt polish (2026-09-26)

The user accepted the flyers and the compies as they were. They said the Triceratops looked like it had "a borderline elephant trunk" and asked for a substantial polish pass on the Safari dinosaurs, which the title screen shows at close range. The trunk was a long, thin, round-tipped snout drooping out of a head drawn at 1.2×. All seven ground sculpts were rebuilt in `scripts/build-safari.mjs`, including the Gallimimus, previously built from primitives. The flyers, compies and lizards are unchanged.

- **Kit:** `placer()` authors each head in its own frame (origin at the neck joint, z along the skull) and places it with a pitch. `carve()` subtracts eye sockets, nostrils and the theropod antorbital hollow. `sheet()` makes thin curved sheets (the Triceratops frill and the Dilophosaurus crests), and `plate(...,{leaf})` makes rounded leaf plates. Sheets never go thinner than 0.6 voxel. The bake throws if a surface reaches its bounds, because 16-bit positions wrap outside them: a Parasaurolophus crest poking out smeared a slab from the ground to the sky.
- **Triceratops:** a deep, narrow face with a hooked rostral beak over a pointed predentary, a short nose horn, long brow horns curving forward, cheek spikes, and a solid frill dished slightly forward. The frill is thickest along its midline and scalloped with 19 epoccipitals; its lower corners come down beside the cheeks. The skull dips 24° nose-down; the body has thigh and shoulder masses and hoofed toes.
- **Stegosaurus:** a short, deep head with cheeks, brow and beak, on a narrowing throat with bony ossicles. The first pass tapered smoothly out of the neck and read as an anteater. 17 rounded leaf plates are tallest over the hips; the thagomizer spikes are longer.
- **Others:** the theropods have a skull and jaw loft with a low brow ridge over a socketed eye, teeth and lip line. The Dilophosaurus snout is kinked, under two thin, tall, barred half-disc crests. The Parasaurolophus has a duck bill, a thicker neck and a continuous tube crest. The Pachycephalosaurus got a larger head and dome, a knobbed squamosal shelf and nasal and cheek knobs. The Gallimimus has a small big-eyed head with a long beak on an S-neck.
- **Hide:** the bake adds mottling, eyes with iris, pupil (a slit for the theropods) and dark lids, darkened nostrils, and softer broad occlusion. The spare bytes carry the shader's inputs: colour alpha is the fine-scale amount (`hide`); the normal's fourth byte is gloss for eyes, horn, beak and hooves (positive) or large scales (negative). `safari-models.js` exposes them as the `hide` and `gloss` attributes.
- **Shader** (`SKIN_GLSL` in `critters.js`): model-space Worley cells give raised fine scales and, on backs and flanks, a pavement of large low scales. The bump is the analytic cell gradient turned into view space. A screen-space `dFdx` bump shaded in 2×2-pixel blocks at title distance, and scattered raised tubercles read as warts. `SKIN` is 0 on the Low tier (no cell evaluation); gloss still applies.
- **Gallimimus:** the baked mesh replaces the primitive geometry and material once the models load, in both Safari and the chase herds. It keeps its chase tuning (centre 0.31, hull, single hit sphere), so `test:wildlife` behaves as before.
- **Budget:** High 24k–59k triangles per species, Low 7k–18k; `safari-runners.bin` is 5.42 MB (3.21 MB gzipped; previously 3.65 / about 2.1). It loads in parallel with the 11.9 MB Rex. Worst-case title frame, with five big animals close to the camera (`art/review/safari3/perf.cjs`, 1600×900, RTX 3070): High 3.28 → 3.56 ms, Low 2.98 → 2.88 ms.
- **Checks:** `test:safari`, `test:wildlife` and `test:smoke` pass. Before and after captures, skinned and clay sheets from four angles plus a head close-up, title views, gait strips, kill falls and both tiers are in ignored `art/review/safari3/` (`look.cjs <tag> <species> sheet,menu`, `kill.cjs`, `perf.cjs`; `pre-*` are the before images).
- **Open:** no modelled skin folds at the joints; the Quetzalcoatlus is still built from primitives (the flyers were accepted as they are).

## Safari Run score attack (2026-09-25, finished and released 2026-09-26)

The user asked for a separate, harmless 90-second mode: shoot as many passing animals as possible, points by species, scores kept in a cookie, extra and very rare species, AAA fidelity, proportionate tests, and publishing straight to production. Codex (Astra) built most of it on 2026-09-25 and ran out of tokens before committing. Claude finished it on 2026-09-26 with the user's extra requests: more dinosaurs, better models and animation, and a Safari title screen without the Rex that shows the hunted wildlife instead. The Rex chase is unchanged and stays in the mode picker. Player-facing rules are in the README's Safari section.

- **Rules** (`safari-rules.js`): 15-species catalogue (points, hits, rarity, hint), three-second ready countdown, 90 s of real active time (separate from the capped physics step), streak within 4 s (`CHAIN`: ×2 from 3, ×3 from 6, ×4 from 10, ×5 from 15), `RANKS`, and the cookie board. `Encounter.startSafari()` selects the rule path; it never schedules attacks, debris, defeat or Rex damage. Results use `result='safari'`.
- **Population** (`safari-director.js`): its own schedule, independent of quality. Common crossings (`COMMON`, weighted `[weight,[kind,count,gap]]`) every 1.7–2.35 s, 1.45–2.1 after 30 s and 1.15–1.8 after 60 s; pterosaurs every 3.5–6 s; birds every 10–15 s; one rare roll every 12–17 s (`RARE`; legendaries 4–5% each; none after 84 s); a stampede at 77 s. The jungle's natural life (roosts, basking lizards, packs flushed by the Jeep) now also runs in Safari as a bonus layer. `parade(dt)` is the title-screen version: the whole roster, nothing scored. A bug to remember: the weighted `pick()` returns only the value, so a table row must hold its payload as one value; an earlier flat row made every common crossing silently spawn nothing.
- **Art** (`scripts/build-safari.mjs` → `public/models/safari-runners.bin`, format 2; superseded in detail by the sculpt polish above): six SDF/loft sculpts at two budgets. Raptor and Dilophosaurus got a tapered, narrower snout and jaw (the first heads read as duck bills); the Dilophosaurus crests are two thin splayed plates (the first read as rabbit ears). New: Pachycephalosaurus (bone dome with a knob ring), Triceratops (head at 1.2×, epoccipital frill, three horns, beak) and Stegosaurus (13 kite plates from an extruded-rhombus SDF, four tail spikes). High 22k–39k triangles, Low 9k–17k. Positions are 16-bit in each model's bounds, the rig is four bytes plus a per-model pivot table, indices 16-bit; `safari-models.js` expands them. The header also carries each species' body centre, hit spheres and a 26-direction resting hull. 3.64 MB raw, about 2.1 MB as served (Pages gzips `.bin`).
- **Rig and motion** (`critters.js` `BAKED`): bipeds use the Gallimimus gait; quadrupeds put `lead` −1 on the fore legs, so the existing phase rule trots them on diagonal pairs, with `swing` scaling the stride (0.56–0.62). Fore legs fold back when a quadruped dies, and it rolls onto its side instead of going head over heels. A non-lethal hit makes the animal flinch (yaw, roll, head) away from the shot and run up to 30% harder. The golden compy (compy kind, gold tint, glint on death) and ghost raptor are colourings with their own speed and swerve. Empty pools are hidden, so they issue no draw.
- **Hits:** baked species test a chain of hit spheres (yaw and scale applied, body sphere keeps the minimum on-screen size) and return the entry point, so blood appears on the hide. Grenades reach them through `(spheres[0].r+.35)*scale` extra radius.
- **Presentation:** `safari-ui.js` (mode picker, remembered in `localStorage` key `rex-pursuit-mode`; field guide with a bagged mark per species; HUD score count-up, multiplier colour steps, kills to the next step; streak and trophy callouts; results count-up, best trophy and next rank). `safari-fx.js` puts kill points in the world (they ride the road with the body and stack when close) and one tag over the wounded animal or else the announced rare, with its remaining hits. Audio: `audio.call(kind)` per species on spawn, `audio.death` per species, `audio.rare()` sting (synthesized interface cue) on a rare sighting and a rare kill.
- **Title screen:** `menuScene()` in `chase.js` hides the Rex (and skips her update and footfall effects) and the brachiosaur while Safari is picked; the director parades the roster and the menu gunner tracks `critters.showcase()`. Picking Rex Pursuit brings her back.
- **Persistence:** one-year `rex_safari_v1` cookie, scoped to the entry's directory, Secure on HTTPS / SameSite=Lax; top five, run count and `seen` (species ever bagged, validated against the catalogue). Local storage is the fallback.
- **Checks:** `test-safari.mjs` (in `test:logic`) and `verify-safari.cjs` (Rex-free title parade, remembered mode, 15-row guide, real raycast kills of ten species including all legendaries and the quadrupeds, grenade damage, pause, results, cookie reload, restart, chase return, touch, phone). `test:smoke` and `test:wildlife` cover the shared critter/flyer code in the chase. A perfect-aim bot (`art/review/safari2/bot.cjs`) finished a run with 212 kills and 210,950 points and no errors; humans will score far less, which the ranks assume. Frame cost at 1600×900 (headless RTX 3070): Safari 7.3 ms Low / 6.9 ms High versus the chase 9.4 / 9.9 ms. Review tools in ignored `art/review/safari2/`: `studio.cjs <tag> studio,menu,play,phone,film`, `density.cjs`, `bot.cjs`, `bot-shots.cjs`.
- **Open:** the quadrupeds' torsos are smooth barrels without skin folds, fine at play distance but plain in a close-up; the Quetzalcoatlus is still built from primitives; physical phone performance is not established by emulation.

## River ford and mud build-up (2026-09-25 release, `80c3af0`)

The user put Drop 7 (photo mode) on permanent hold and asked for two backlog items instead: a river ford where the Jeep and the Rex throw spray and leave wet banks, and mud and wetness building up on the Rex over the chase.

- **Where the ford comes from:** `river.js` defines the channel (centre line, half-width, bed, carve) in chunk-local coordinates and the shared uniforms (`FORD`). `environment.js` builds a seventh layout on carved ground (`fordHeight`) and exposes `jungle.ford.place(z)`/`release()`, `groundAt` (ford-aware), `fordDip` and `waterDepth`. `ford.js` schedules it once per chase (45 m of pursuit travel, never during the detour), and owns the water surface, spray, wakes, the wet-ground map and the splash hooks. `rexChase.ford.stage(z)` lays it out immediately for reviews.
- **Ground follow:** `jeep.ground` lifts and pitches the Jeep from its four tyres; `updateCamera` adds that lift and 60% of the pitch; `gait.ground`/`gait.water` put her contacts and body on the bed and raise her step. All return 0 away from the ford, so nothing else moves.
- **Rex coat:** `rex-coat.js` writes `uRexCoat` (splatter level, rinse level and height, dryness) and `uRexSoak` (river soak and line, runoff phase, rain soak). The start state matches the accepted look (compare `art/review/ford/coat-base-clear.png` with `coat-a-start.png`). The new high-frequency noise uses a sin-free hash (`rexNoiseS`) for phone precision.
- **Also changed:** `RAIN_TIME` now wraps at 600 s (the puddle ripples and the river take a bounded clock). The soft-smoke capacity went to 204 for the 64-puff spindrift pool.
- **Reviews and checks:** `art/review/ford/capture.cjs [conditions] [tag] [first|third]` (crossing stills), `coat.cjs` (coat stages on a frozen menu Rex), `perf.cjs` (crossing vs road), `scripts/verify-ford.cjs` (`npm run test:ford`).
- **Release:** the full gate passed before the push; after the deploy, live `test:pages` and `test:ford` (with `TEST_URL`) passed.
- **Open:** the victory fall still assumes flat ground, so if she dies in the river she lies on the surface for the second or two before she slides out. The spray reads well in third person and at night; in first person the gun hides her feet, so the plumes are what show.

## Visual overhaul (2026-09-23)

The user asked for a studio-grade look, with a stunning Rex that stays playable on common devices, and gave creative latitude. They then asked specifically that the Rex not look washed out or too shiny, and that the inside of the mouth look normal and high fidelity when she roars. Gameplay rules, timings, rig motion, cinematics and the throat interior are unchanged.

- **Frame pipeline** (`src/chase/post.js`): the frame loop renders into an MSAA HalfFloat target, then ray-marched sun scattering (canopy mask + real shadow map), a 13-tap/tent dual-filter bloom, and a final pass (ACES, grade, vignette, grain, mild chromatic edge, sharpen under dynamic resolution). Only `renderFrame()` routes through it; direct `renderer.render()` calls (treeline masks, Water reflections, swallow composite) still work. The swallow composite draws after it to the default framebuffer. Without float render targets the game falls back to direct rendering.
- **Atmosphere** (`src/chase/atmosphere.js`): procedural sky dome with drifting clouds; a PMREM environment built from a sky + forest-band dome (replaces `RoomEnvironment`); height-attenuated fog with a warm sun lobe installed into `ShaderChunk` before any material compiles. `createCanopy()` builds one tileable leaf-gap mask used by an invisible shadow-casting plane (dappled light on everything), the volumetric shafts and the sunbeam motes, so shafts line up with ground dapples. **three filters shadow casters by the main camera's layers**, so the caster stays on layer 0 with `colorWrite:false` rather than on a separate layer.

There is also a dim cool rim from behind the animal, a soft fill from the Jeep side and a green/earth hemisphere. **The user found the first overhaul pass hard to see**: with the sun behind her she was a backlit silhouette against bright haze. Do not move the sun behind the Rex. The canopy caster is offset along the sun direction so the open road corridor in the mask lands on the road; post and motes subtract the same `canopy.offset`. Direct light she loses to cast shadow is partly restored on her hide (`(1-sh)*.42`) so tree shade never swallows her; volumetric density (.0085) and fog (.0138) were thinned so she is not veiled. The rim is deliberately low (0.6): at 1.25 its specular sparkled on the brow's fine scale detail. The victory arrival swaps to open-sky values and disables the canopy; restart restores `jungleLighting()`.
- **Rainforest** (`src/chase/foliage.js`, `environment.js`): all procedural. Buttressed giants with lianas and card crowns, leaning edge trees that close a canopy tunnel, palms, tree ferns, ground ferns, elephant-ear/banana leaves, lumpy mossy rocks, fallen logs and clumped instanced grass on a graded ground mesh (road ruts, verge, leaf litter, wet patches). Roughness keeps the accepted `.48-.86` remap, then the dry hide has a `.62` floor outside the lips and mouth. Eyes gained a limbal ring and blinks (`gaze.blink()` on hits); teeth get stained roots; the cornea became a pure additive reflection; the ribcage breathes via `ribCage_*` bones (`Ctrl_breath` has no skin influence).

Auto picks from the GPU string and device class; a frame-time governor adjusts render scale and, on sustained strain at minimum scale, drops one tier. Menu and Pause have a picker (stored in `localStorage`, key `rex-pursuit-quality`) with a readout.
- **Front end** (`index.html`, `src/chase-premium.css`): boot screen with real load progress, a staged title reveal, a slow menu camera drift, glass HUD panels and a segmented boss bar. Test-facing ids, touch-target sizes and layouts are unchanged; the controls tip hides on desktops under 861 px tall to make room for the picker.

It previously aimed at a fixed depth along the camera ray while rounds flew to the real impact, so tracers left at an angle to the barrel, obviously so in third person. Yaw/pitch damping went from 15/14 to 26. The measured barrel-vs-shot angle is ≤0.3° in first person and ≤2° in third (`art/review/aim-check.cjs`). - **Footfall stomp** is a vertical-only damped spring on the camera (`stompOffset`), not random `shake`. Random lateral shake moved the first-person/phone framing enough to push challenge targets under the HUD in `test:pressure`.

- Relative cost on this RTX 3070 laptop, headless Chrome, 1600×900 pursuit (`art/review/perf-tiers.cjs`): roughly 4-5 ms/frame across tiers with ~290-330 draw calls. That is not a physical-phone measurement; the governor and Low tier exist for weaker devices.

## Product direction

The concept evolved from a stationary encounter into a first-person mounted-gun chase, with a third-person camera showing the park-themed Jeep and its left-seat driver. Keep the cinematic sense of scale, readable arcade targets, and believable creature movement. The user notices small problems in feet, knees, hands, eye focus, jaw timing, and close-up materials.

The main accepted features are:

- A stopped Jeep opening: the Rex breaks through the right-hand jungle, roars, and follows as the vehicle accelerates. Moving roars retain locomotion; audible Rex vocals drive the jaw from their actual audio envelope.
- A 90-second active pursuit deadline with ordered targets, increasing pressure, and branches broken by physical Rex contact before they fly toward the Jeep. No special headshot text or color.
- A midpoint escape feint into the left jungle and a close return from that same side. Concealment comes from foliage along a continuous route, not hiding or teleporting the model. The road verge stays relatively open and vegetation grows denser farther away.
- Persistent bullet/explosion damage, including the face. Skin, wounds, tongue, and eyes have distinct finishes.
- An animated gun feed, ejected cases and links, articulated reload hands, a left-hand steering wheel, and a safari ranger driver visible in third person.
- Touch aiming above the thumb, independent aim/fire pointers, accessible reload/grenade controls, and suppression of selection and long-press callouts.
- A flexible, weighted Rex death fall on victory. Player defeat instead forces first person, knocks the gun away, spins the Jeep once, and ends with the Rex walking up and swallowing the player.
